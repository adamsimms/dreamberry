## Démarche artistique / Artistic approach (max 250)

*Cut of the Eastern Edge 2026-09 statement. Community, family as collaborators, debt. The window itself stays in the 400w field. No stacked short sentences.*

Pinchard's Island is a treeless outport, a small fishing community reached by water, off the northeast coast of Ktaqmkuk (Newfoundland). My grandmother was born and raised there. In the 1960s a government resettlement program emptied the island: residents floated their houses across the ocean until only a few gravestones remained. Years later my extended family began going back and built an off-grid cabin. My practice is rooted in those returns, often with my family as collaborators.

Distance is the material of my work. I am from Newfoundland, and I have lived away for half my life. I am unsettled, and I am resettled. I long to be present in places that history has tried to erase, and to understand which parts of intangible culture are portable and which are rooted in place.

I work with photography, video, sculpture, and microelectronics. Cloudberry fixed a solar camera to our cabin and held one window until the light failed. Cabin rebuilt a Newfoundland saltbox hearth in a Montreal studio, with that feed as its window. Driftwood rose and fell on live buoy data. In each of them presence is partial, remote, and permitted to fail.

I keep asking machines to hold a place. Pinchard's Island is Ktaqmkuk, and an apparatus that invents presence on resettled, colonized ground is not innocent of the erasure that made the outport possible. I hold that in the work rather than resolve it.

Adam Simms is a media artist from Ktaqmkuk (Newfoundland), based in Tiohtià:ke (Montreal). Using cameras, data, and microelectronics, he creates works that treat the environment and cultural memory as interconnected material.

For over a decade his work has centred on Pinchard's Island, the outport his grandmother resettled from in the 1960s. Cloudberry, Cabin, and Driftwood used solar cameras and live data to carry dialect, place-names, and shoreline knowledge that do not travel cleanly into rooms far from the island.

His work has appeared in Riddle Fence and Ciel Variable, and been exhibited at the Leonard and Bina Ellen Art Gallery and Occurrence. He holds a BFA and an MFA from Concordia University, where he has taught as an Artist-in-Residence.

He is building Dreamberry, a dreamed view of that cabin window.

## Description détaillée du projet / Detailed artistic and technological description (max 400)

*Residency plan. B1: how little resolution can still be presence. Lab: two-pane field, Cloudberry sleep, dim on a panel. Dreaming already runs. D: I live in Montréal, not on the island. K: workshop + open studio. Contract hours, GPU cadence, local dreamer: tech notes, not this field. No re:site, no Arteles, no URL, no frame counts, no green AI, no exhibition venue. No "honest room."*

Cloudberry was a solar camera I built on my family's cabin on Pinchard's Island. It photographed the same north-facing window once an hour until the cold and the short days drew more power than the sun could return and it went dark.

Dreamberry is that camera still dreaming. A custom model generates the window it can no longer photograph, trained only on photographs from that Cloudberry system. Live weather from the island shapes the dreamed image, so the atmosphere is true while the rocks are memory and invention. Every dreamed view is fed back into the system, so the model trains on itself and grows less certain until it collapses. The model is restricted to a single view and allowed to forget. I do not know how long the place stays recoverable, and that is part of the work.

I've trained the preliminary model and it has begun to dream. This residency is focused on fabricating Dreamberry's window, and asks how little resolution can still hold the place. Portrait LED panels form a diptych, with a gap between them, echoing the original window of the cabin. Frosted acrylic covers the panels so light falls softly on their surface and on the surrounding wall. Whoever pauses long enough watches one dream give way to the next as live weather arrives. The diptych runs on a solar panel and a battery, without gallery electricity, under the same constraint as the original camera. When the battery is thin the window sleeps.

The outcomes of the residency are the solar-powered window and its system; a record of how much of the place still holds; and an artist talk and an open studio. I would also invite other OBORO staff and residency artists to collaborate on a roundtable on AI ethics, environmental limits, and model collapse.
